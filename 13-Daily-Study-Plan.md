# Daily Study Operating Guide

Open `npm run dev` → **Today**. It uses explicit phase/week, weekly focus,
bounded revision and carry-forward. Start in under two minutes: choose today's
mode, read why the main task was selected, and start. Close with the actual
outcome and one gap; normal evidence entry should take under 30 seconds.
[TODAY.md](./TODAY.md) remains a manual fallback.

## Reusable time modes

These are budgets, not compulsory blocks. Choose one main task; rotate DSA and
technical/practical emphasis using weekly focus rather than every track every day.
Speaking uses the **same** topic and is included in these budgets.

| Mode | Main task | Revision | Speaking | Save / buffer | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Minimum day | Optional | 5–10 min retrieval | 5–10 min shortened speak/check/retry | 1–2 min | About 11–22 min |
| 90-minute day | 50 min DSA or technical/practical | 15 min | 15 min | 10 min | 90 min |
| 120-minute day | 60 min primary + 20 min same-focus practice | 15 min | 15 min | 10 min | 120 min |
| 150-minute day | 75 min primary + 25 min practical/technical | 20 min | 20 min | 10 min | 150 min |
| Weekend / extended | One deeper practical/design task or timed round | One bounded revision | 20–30 min topic/project speaking | Breaks, tests and reflection | 3–5 hours only when possible |

The usual speaking loop is [15 minutes](./english/README.md#daily-15-minute-speaking-loop).
Minimum mode shortens it on difficult days. Rest/emergency days are allowed;
there is no requirement to study through illness or an incident.

## Solve, verify and explain

Read the prompt before a solution. Try for roughly 25–35 minutes; take a small
hint when stuck, retry, then study the approach if needed. Record **hinted** or
**studied solution** honestly. Finish with approach, invariant, example, time/space
complexity, edge cases and one lesson in the coding file.

Save/test in DSA, explain aloud, then **Save attempt**. **Mark Complete** is
separate and validated; failed/studied attempts do not satisfy completion.
Available tests must pass; otherwise self-certify what you actually checked.
Topic retrieval/practical and JS Core/English explanations have their own saves.
A technically correct answer can still have a partial explanation.

The existing [dependency queue](./01-DSA-Questions.md#dependency-ordered-practice-queue)
is the fallback. DSA focus selects within that queue, retaining order inside
matching topics; it does not certify prerequisites. Confirm basics before choosing
a later pattern. Challenge/stretch work is optional; library completion is not a gate.

## Revision day

Use **Revision** for one coding review **or** up to three short retrievals.
Dates derive from outcomes and delayed recall (1 → 3 → 7 → 14 → 30 days), rather
than five manual columns for every problem. Failed/unclear answers return sooner.
Same-day retries do not grow intervals. Defer extra due work and reduce new
scope at review. Historical completion is optional baseline retrieval, not
mastery or overdue evidence.

## Mock day

Replace the main task with a timed round; do not add it on top. Use
[Mock Interviews](./Mock-Interviews/README.md). Early phases use short explanations
and familiar timed coding; later add 45-minute coding/design, practical and
behavioral rounds. Retain a weekly 30–45 minute communication session when feasible:
introduction, project, technical questions, debugging and behavioral follow-up.
Record DSA mocks in the workspace; broader mock history remains manual/read-only.
Never invent timing, independence or past experience.

## Weekly review day

Use **Weekly Review** when your study week ends (about 15–20 minutes within the
weekly budget). Attempts, reviews, explanations, recorded time, gaps and due
work are generated. Decide capacity, focus, carry-forward and what to stop.
Choose Continue / Consolidate / Advance using
[the roadmap policy](./00-Roadmap.md#weekly-review-and-advancement).
No calendar date advances the learning week. Save a concrete first session step.

## Busy-week and carry-forward rules

- Enter reduced capacity or Consolidate after a difficult week; preserve recall
  and speaking when feasible. New problems can wait.
- Carry one essential main item and optionally one small secondary; replace the
  list at review rather than accumulating tasks.
- Reschedule intentionally, revise, deprioritize or drop the rest. Never cram
  two missed days into one or double next week's workload.
- Stretch work is not a prerequisite. Rest/low energy are allowed; streaks are
  context, not a planning gate.
- If technically sound but unclear in English, reuse those topics for speaking;
  do not add a separate syllabus or long grammar block.
